# Where SmartHomeDash stands, 13 November 2026

A status report at version 0.8.16, written to plan what comes next. The plan itself is [plan.md](plan.md); the research behind it is [research-2026-11.md](research-2026-11.md).

## In one paragraph

SmartHomeDash went from first public release (0.4.0, 8 November) to 0.8.16 in five days, through 25 published releases. The **0.9 Foundation** milestone is complete. Of **1.0 Nearly zero setup**, four of seven tasks are done: quick start, report a problem, editor comforts, and backup and restore. What is left of 1.0 is what widens the audience: a card for other dashboards, more languages, and a listing in Home Assistant's new built-in Marketplace (which replaced HACS in 2026.11). Nothing in 1.1 or 1.2 has started. The product is feature-rich for its age. The risks now lie in reach (0 stars, 1 issue, no forum presence yet), the move from HACS to the Marketplace, and one test that fails on a second machine.

## Quality

- **Size:** about 9,000 lines (Python backend 1,330, frontend JavaScript 5,900, strings 1,050, CSS 670). The biggest files are `ui/setup.js` (837 lines) and `i18n.js` (1,053).
- **Tests:** 68 backend tests and 54 browser tests (both examples at five sizes, editing, prices, names, quick start, backup, shapes, report, and a performance budget). CI requires hassfest, the HACS check, a module syntax check and the tests.
- **Performance (measured here, 4× CPU throttling, large test home):** 910 DOM nodes on the overview, first render 923 ms, longest task 116 ms. The budget is 4,000 nodes and 2 s. The longest task is over the 100 ms per-task line, but within the averaged rule the test now uses.
- **One failing test on a second machine:** "pinch zoom with two fingers, and a tap still works after" failed 3 of 3 runs here (Playwright 1.63.0, same as CI) while CI passes. The tap on **Reset zoom** right after a one-finger drag is lost. 0.8.13 called this a test timing issue and added a wait, but it may be a real lost tap on slower devices (see Q6 in the plan).
- **Gaps in testing:** the backend runs against stand-ins for Home Assistant, not a real instance. The config flow, panel registration, diagnostics and the update action have never run under Home Assistant in CI (see Q7).

## What changed around it since the first research (10 November)

- **HACS is now the Home Assistant Marketplace (2026.11).** A separate HACS install is blocked. Custom repositories are added with "Add from link" after a GitHub sign-in, and project pages show stars, installs and brand images [R27][R4]. The README and setup guide still describe the old HACS menus.
- **HA's own dashboards target the whole family.** The default Overview is less cluttered, areas drive climate and energy views, and user profiles carry personal themes [R28][R29]. Community sections can now be added to HA dashboards [R4].
- **Ready-made dashboard apps are rising** over assembling cards (GlassHome, Magic Frame with a photo frame mode, Tunet with energy and EV cards). Reviewers point out that guest access is missing in most of them [R30].
- **AI on dashboards:** camera event timelines with AI summaries (LLM Vision) and LLM-written daily or weather briefings are now common add-ons [R31][R32]. Home Assistant also ships an MCP server [R41].
- **Low-power displays:** e-ink status boards (TRMNL, ESPHome e-paper, Kindles) for calendars and home status, refreshed every few minutes [R33][R34].
- **Swedish capacity fees:** the government stopped the requirement that all grid companies introduce capacity tariffs by 2027. The 30–50 companies that already have them keep them [R35], so a peak tracker helps some users but not all.
- **Energy planning:** solar forecasts (Forecast.Solar, Solcast) and battery planners (Predbat, EMHASS) are popular next to live flow [R36][R37].
- **Accessibility:** users ask for keyboard navigation, readable graphs and larger text [R39][R40].
- **Summer houses:** frost, humidity, intrusion and history are what holiday-home setups watch [R38]. That is the owner's own Singö case.

## Strengths to keep

- The only real-photo picture of the home that is set up in the UI, in about an hour, with no modelling.
- Deep Home Assistant integration: registry names, areas, floors, automatic placement.
- Fast on old tablets, with a measured budget.
- Energy on the picture, with Nordic prices done right (quarter-hours, the core Nord Pool action).
- Privacy by design (report-a-problem, diagnostics, no outside calls).

## Risks and gaps

1. **Reach.** Nobody outside the owner knows the project yet: no Marketplace listing, no forum thread, panel only, two languages.
2. **Install docs are out of date** after the Marketplace change, which hurts first impressions right when people arrive.
3. **One test is unstable** outside CI, and the backend has never run under a real Home Assistant.
4. **Large files:** `setup.js` and `i18n.js` slow down every future change and will grow with languages.
5. **Missing pieces users expect** from the research: people per room, cameras, appliances, guest mode, a screensaver, accessibility.
6. **The owner's own use case,** a summer house watched from afar, is not served as a whole yet: frost, leaks, humidity, warm-up before arrival, both homes at a glance.

## Recommended order for the next stretch

1. **M1** Marketplace docs and metadata, and **Q6** the lost tap. Small, and they protect first impressions and trust in the tests.
2. **Q7** real Home Assistant in CI, and **Q8** split `setup.js` and the strings, before S5 adds five languages to them.
3. **S4** card, **S5** languages, **S6** Marketplace listing, **A1** accessibility, **G1** launch. This completes 1.0 and brings people in.
4. **F1–F2** summer house mode, which serves the owner directly. Then **W2** guest mode and **W1** screensaver, which reviewers find missing elsewhere.
5. **1.1** on the picture (people, openings in place, appliances, cameras, power), with **E1** solar forecast next to the flow.
